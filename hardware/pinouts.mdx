Connector and pad reference for the Delta GaN ESC. All four interfaces — servo/PPM, CAN, UART, and USB-C — sit on the same edge of the board.

<Warning>
  **Check the pin order against the board before wiring.** Signal order reverses depending on which side you are looking from — see [reading the pin order](#reading-the-pin-order) below. Confirm against the silkscreen with the board in front of you.
</Warning>

## Reading the pin order

Each connector is listed twice below — once as seen from the **connector face**, and once as seen from the **board side** the connector is accessible from. The two are the same physical connector read in opposite directions.

<Warning>
  The two reference views **number pins from opposite ends**. Rather than trust an absolute "pin 1", identify the connector's orientation on your board and match the *signal sequence* to the corresponding view below.

  The reliable landmark: on both the CAN and UART connectors, **GND sits at one end and the 3.3 V or NC pin at the other.**
</Warning>

## Signal connectors

### Servo / PPM

2-pin JST-SH 1.0. Accessible from the **MCU side**. This is the PPM input configured in [input configuration](/guides/input-configuration).

| View | Direction | Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connector face | left → right | GND, SIGNAL / PPM |
| MCU side | top → bottom | SIGNAL / PPM, GND |

### CAN

4-pin JST-SH 1.0. Accessible from the **MCU side**. Used for [UAVCAN / DroneCAN](/guides/uavcan) and VESC-native CAN.

| View | Direction | Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connector face | left → right | GND, CANL, CANH, 3.3 V |
| MCU side | top → bottom | 3.3 V, CANH, CANL, GND |

<Note>
  The CAN connector carries a **3.3 V** rail, not 5 V. It is not a general-purpose supply — see [auxiliary power budget](#auxiliary-power-budget) before drawing anything from it.

  Bus termination is also not documented. A CAN bus needs 120 Ω at each physical end; confirm whether the Delta provides it before relying on it.
</Note>

### UART

4-pin, **3.3 V logic**. Accessible from the **component side**.

| View | Direction | Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connector face | left → right | GND, TX / SCL, RX / SDA, Ex — NC |
| Component side | top → bottom | Ex — NC, RX / SDA, TX / SCL, GND |

<Note>
  The data pins are **shared between UART and I2C** — TX doubles as SCL, RX as SDA. Which function is active depends on firmware configuration; the pins cannot serve both at once.

  `Ex` is not connected.
</Note>

<Warning>
  UART is **3.3 V logic**. Do not connect a 5 V device directly without level shifting.
</Warning>

### USB-C

Configuration, telemetry, and firmware update. Accessible from the **component side**.

<Warning>
  **USB is data only — the Delta is not powered over USB.** It will not boot or enumerate until DC power is applied to the main terminals. Use a USB A → USB C cable. See the [quickstart](/guides/quickstart).
</Warning>

## Power and phase

Large solder pads, exposed on both sides of the board.

<Warning>
  High voltage and high current. Up to **93 V continuous** input and **70 A continuous** output with forced airflow. Never make or break these connections under load, and use an anti-spark connector on the battery side.
</Warning>

| Pad | Signal | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **VIN +** | Battery positive | 3–12S |
| **GND** | Battery negative | Main current return |
| **U** | Phase U | Solder motor leads direct to pad |
| **V** | Phase V | Solder motor leads direct to pad |
| **W** | Phase W | Solder motor leads direct to pad |

<Note>
  Phase order determines direction of rotation. Prefer flipping the direction toggle in Motor Settings over swapping phase leads — see [motor tuning](/guides/motor-tuning).
</Note>

## Auxiliary power budget

The Delta's internal rails are supplied by an **LM5163** buck regulator rated at **500 mA total**. That budget covers the controller's own logic and gate drive as well as anything you draw off the board.

<Warning>
  **Do not draw more than 100 mA from any output.** The board's auxiliary power capability is limited, and loads above 100 mA are discouraged.

  The 500 mA figure is the regulator's total capability, **not** headroom available to you. The controller's own consumption comes out of it first.
</Warning>

Practically, this means the Delta should not be treated as a power source for anything substantial:

| Load | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| CAN transceiver on a peer node | Generally fine, well under 100 mA |
| RC receiver | Give it its own supply |
| Companion computer | Give it its own supply |
| Servos | Never — give them their own supply |

<Note>
  Browning out the 3.3 V rail takes the controller's logic down with it. On a moving vehicle that means losing motor control, so keep external draw conservative rather than close to the limit.
</Note>

## Serial number

Each board carries a unit ID on the component side, next to the UART connector (for example `0007`). **Quote this for support and RMA.**

### NDAA compliance

Serial numbers prefixed with **`N`** — for example `N0007` — identify **NDAA-compliant** boards.

<Note>
  If NDAA compliance is a requirement for your programme, check the prefix on the physical board. The marking on the unit is the authoritative record.
</Note>
